The four headings follow the course: Do not be picky with the numbers; Be aware of biases; Keep the graphs clear and non-confusing; Encourage third-party review.

Evidence notes distinguish observed labels/geometry from verified underlying statistics. Figure 7's November 8.6% label appears inconsistent with its position near 9.0% and above March 8.8%. Its zoomed scale and filled area/perspective are discussed with care: a justified non-zero line axis is not automatically misleading. Figure 8's approximately 4% bar baseline distorts length ratios. Printed-label arithmetic gives 7/4.8 ≈ 1.46 versus idealised above-baseline lengths 3/0.8 = 3.75; this is not a pixel measurement or computed screenshot lie factor. Unequal year gaps and 2023/2027 being future relative to the June 2021 broadcast caption require clear status/context. The cropped image does not establish a percentage denominator, realised future outcomes, data provenance or deliberate broadcaster intent.

Supplementary primary references are ONS axes guidance and its line-chart discussion. The optional lie-factor teaching uses defined relative effects and an original 100→110, baseline-90 mathematical example; zero data effect is undefined. The course's 0.95–1.05 suggestion is identified as course guidance, not truthfulness certification. Descriptive charts do not universally require inferential statistical significance. Relevant subsets can be valid with transparent scope; planned peer review is distinct from observed feedback.
