## Step 1: The hypothetical business

**Nordtre AS** — sustainable home and office furniture.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | Sustainable home and office furniture |
| **Founded** | 2021, Trondheim, Norway |
| **Products** | Flat-pack desks, shelving and chairs in FSC-certified Norwegian timber, plus refinishing and spare-part kits |
| **Target market** | Urban Norwegian households and small businesses, ages 28–45, environmentally motivated, largely home-office buyers |
| **Average headcount** | 34 (production and warehouse 19, design and commercial 15) |
| **Currency** | NOK |
| **Period** | FY2026, compared against FY2025 |

The business is deliberately built with **three channels of very different economics**, because that is what makes the KPI analysis in Step 4 non-trivial. D2C is high volume at low order value; B2B contract work is low volume at high contract value; retail partnerships carry the thinnest margin because the partner takes a share.

## Step 2: Financial KPIs

### 2a. Three sources of sales revenue

| Source | Revenue (NOK) | Share | Note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| D2C e-commerce | 7,200,000 | 39.1% | 3,000 orders × NOK 2,400 average order value |
| Retail partnerships | 6,900,000 | 37.5% | Interior stores on wholesale terms |
| B2B contract sales | 4,300,000 | 23.4% | Office fit-outs for small and medium businesses |
| **Total Revenue** | **18,400,000** | **100%** | |

### 2b. Profit margins

| Line | Amount (NOK) | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| Total Revenue | 18,400,000 | Sum of the three channels |
| Cost of goods sold | 10,120,000 | Timber, hardware, manufacturing |
| **Gross Profit** | **8,280,000** | Revenue − COGS |
| **Gross Margin** | **45.0%** | Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100 |
| Marketing | 2,300,000 | |
| Salaries and personnel | 3,100,000 | |
| Logistics and fulfilment | 640,000 | |
| Other operating costs | 400,000 | Rent, software, insurance |
| Total operating expenses | 6,440,000 | |
| Total Expenses | 16,560,000 | COGS + operating expenses |
| **Operating Profit (EBIT)** | **1,840,000** | Revenue − Total Expenses |
| **Operating Margin** | **10.0%** | EBIT ÷ Revenue × 100 |
| Corporate tax at 22% | 404,800 | |
| **Net Profit** | **1,435,200** | EBIT − tax |
| **Net Margin** | **7.8%** | Net Profit ÷ Revenue × 100 |

**Why three margins and not one.** Gross, operating and net margin differ here by a factor of nearly six: 45.0%, 10.0% and 7.8%. They answer different questions — respectively, how profitable the product is, how well the company is run, and what the owners keep. A target, a bonus scheme or a channel comparison stated as "profit margin" is ambiguous until the variant is named, and comparing one team's gross margin against another's net margin is a comparison of nothing.

### 2c. Return on Investment

Nordtre committed **NOK 3,000,000** in Q1 FY2026 to a CNC production line and a webshop replatform. The incremental operating profit attributable to that investment is **NOK 780,000** per year.

| Measure | Value | Calculation |
|---|---:|---|
| ROI, year 1 | **26.0%** | 780,000 ÷ 3,000,000 × 100 |
| Payback period | **3.85 years** | 3,000,000 ÷ 780,000 |
| Cumulative ROI over 3 years | **−22.0%** | (780,000 × 3 − 3,000,000) ÷ 3,000,000 × 100 |

Two method choices are deliberate and both are defensible in an evaluation:

1. **The numerator is incremental profit, not total company profit.** Dividing whole-business net profit by one investment credits that investment with everything the company did, including revenue it had nothing to do with. It is the single most common way a reported ROI is inflated.
2. **Both horizons are shown.** A year-1 ROI of +26% looks unambiguously good. The same investment has a cumulative three-year ROI of −22%, because payback is not reached until year 3.85. Neither figure is wrong; quoting only one is. **An ROI without a stated horizon is not an evaluable number.**

## Step 3: Non-financial KPIs

### 3a. Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC is reported **by segment**, because Nordtre acquires two entirely different kinds of customer.

**FY2026**

| Segment | Acquisition spend | New customers | CAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D2C | 890,000 | 8,900 | NOK 100 |
| B2B contract | 700,000 | 140 | NOK 5,000 |
| **Blended** | **1,590,000** | **9,040** | **NOK 175.88** |

**FY2025**

| Segment | Acquisition spend | New customers | CAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D2C | 651,000 | 6,200 | NOK 105 |
| B2B contract | 924,000 | 210 | NOK 4,400 |
| **Blended** | **1,575,000** | **6,410** | **NOK 245.71** |

Blended CAC fell **28.4%** year on year. On its own this reads as a marketing triumph. It is not, and Step 4 explains why.

Note also that blended CAC is **total spend ÷ total customers**, not the average of the two segment CACs. Averaging NOK 100 and NOK 5,000 would give NOK 2,550, which is wrong by a factor of fourteen, because one segment has 64 times the volume of the other. The workbook includes a reconciliation check for exactly this error.

**Lifetime value, because CAC alone is not interpretable**

| Segment | Avg order/contract | Gross margin | Purchases per lifetime | LTV | LTV:CAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D2C | NOK 2,400 | 45% | 1.6 | NOK 1,728 | **17.3×** |
| B2B contract | NOK 78,000 | 38% | 2.2 | NOK 65,208 | **13.0×** |

Both ratios are comfortably above the 3× rule of thumb, so acquisition is healthy in both segments taken separately.

### 3b. Website traffic monitoring

| Metric | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Monthly sessions | 21,500 | 15,600 | **+37.8%** |
| Monthly unique visitors | 14,800 | 11,200 | +32.1% |
| Average session duration | 3.4 min | 3.9 min | −12.8% |
| Monthly D2C orders | 250 | 207 | +20.8% |
| **Conversion rate** | **1.16%** | **1.33%** | **−12.4%** |

**Monitoring strategies:**

- **Track sessions, unique visitors and session duration separately**, never as one "traffic" number. They measure activity, reach and depth respectively, and here they move by different amounts.
- **Segment by source** — organic, paid, email, referral, social — so that a rise in total traffic can be attributed. Paid traffic that does not convert is a cost, not growth.
- **Take orders from the order system, not from analytics.** Analytics conversion tracking is lossy, and reconciling two independent sources is what catches tagging errors.
- **Always report conversion rate alongside traffic.** Traffic is an input; conversion is what turns it into revenue.
- **Set monthly targets on conversion and revenue per session**, not on sessions, so the target cannot be met by buying low-intent traffic.

### 3c. Employee turnover

| Measure | Departures | Avg headcount | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Company total | 7 | 34 | 20.6% |
| Production and warehouse | 5 | 19 | 26.3% |
| Design and commercial | 2 | 15 | 13.3% |
| **Regretted departures only** | **4** | **34** | **11.8%** |

**Contributing factors:**

- **Production and warehouse:** burnout during seasonal launch peaks, physically demanding shift patterns, limited progression path from warehouse into production planning.
- **Design and commercial:** both leavers were **senior product designers** who left for larger studios citing lack of ownership over the product roadmap.

**Mitigation strategies:**

| Factor | Strategy | Which KPI should confirm it worked |
|---|---|---|
| Launch-peak burnout | Level production planning across the quarter; use temporary staff for peaks rather than overtime | Overtime hours per head; regretted turnover in production |
| No progression path | Formal upskilling route from warehouse to production planning, with a named mentor | Internal promotion rate; regretted turnover |
| Designer ownership | Give each senior designer roadmap ownership of a product line | Regretted turnover in design; time-to-launch of new lines |
| Late detection | Quarterly engagement survey, and exit interviews classified as regretted or unregretted | Engagement score as a leading indicator |

**The financial consequence.** At an estimated NOK 95,000 per replacement (recruitment plus training plus lost productivity), seven departures cost **NOK 665,000** — equal to **36.1% of operating profit**. Turnover is not only an HR matter; it is a margin lever.

## Step 4: Analysis — how the KPIs interact

### Interaction 1: CAC ↔ customer mix ↔ lifetime value

This is the most important finding in the analysis.

| | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Blended CAC | NOK 245.71 | NOK 175.88 | **−28.4%** |
| New customers | 6,410 | 9,040 | +41.0% |
| B2B new accounts | 210 | 140 | **−33.3%** |
| B2B CAC | NOK 4,400 | NOK 5,000 | **+13.6%** |
| **Total lifetime value acquired** | **NOK 24,407,280** | **NOK 24,508,320** | **+0.4%** |

Blended CAC fell 28% while the **total lifetime value acquired was flat**. The improvement is a **mix artefact**, not an efficiency gain: acquisition shifted toward cheap, low-LTV D2C customers while high-value B2B new accounts fell by a third and became 14% more expensive to win.

Nordtre acquired 41% more customers for essentially the same future value. Reporting "CAC improved 28%" without the mix would be a materially misleading statement, and it is exactly the failure mode that makes CAC uninterpretable without LTV.

### Interaction 2: Traffic ↔ conversion ↔ revenue

Sessions rose 37.8% but conversion fell 12.4%, so orders rose only 20.8%. Roughly **45% of the traffic growth produced no revenue**. Falling session duration points the same way: the additional visitors are lower-intent.

Website traffic is the classic **vanity metric**. It is a leading indicator, so its value depends entirely on whether the outcome it predicts actually follows. Here the leading indicator improved sharply while its lagging partner improved half as much — a broken predictive link, which is a finding in itself rather than a success.

### Interaction 3: Turnover ↔ cost ↔ margin

NOK 665,000 of replacement cost is 36.1% of operating profit. A non-financial KPI with a direct, quantifiable claim on the financial ones.

### Interaction 4: Turnover ↔ product pipeline ↔ D2C revenue

Design turnover is only 13.3%, well below the company average — and both leavers were senior product designers. The low departmental rate **hides** the risk. New product lines drive D2C traffic and orders, so this is a leading indicator of a future revenue problem that no financial KPI will show for another 12–18 months.

This is why regretted turnover must be separated from unregretted, and why segmenting by team matters more than the headline rate.

### Which KPIs are most crucial, and why

| Rank | KPI | Why it is crucial for Nordtre | Why the obvious alternative is weaker |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LTV:CAC by segment** | The only form in which acquisition efficiency is interpretable, and the only one that exposes the mix shift | Blended CAC moved 28% for reasons unrelated to efficiency |
| 2 | **Gross margin by channel** | Channel mix is the main driver of blended margin, and the three channels differ sharply | Blended gross margin can hold steady while a profitable channel shrinks and a thin one grows |
| 3 | **D2C conversion rate** | The intermediary between traffic paid for and revenue earned | Traffic alone rose 37.8% while contributing far less than that to revenue |
| 4 | **Regretted turnover, by team** | A leading indicator for the product pipeline that D2C revenue depends on | Company-wide turnover of 20.6% averages away the two departures that matter most |

Operating margin and ROI remain essential for reporting and for capital decisions, but they are **lagging**: by the time they move, the causes above have been running for a year. The four KPIs listed are the ones that let Nordtre act while acting is still possible.

## Step 5: Report

### Nordtre AS — KPI summary FY2026

Nordtre AS is a sustainability-driven Norwegian furniture company with three revenue channels of materially different economics. Revenue reached NOK 18.4 million at a 10.0% operating margin. The headline KPIs look healthy; two of them are misleading once decomposed, and that decomposition is the substance of this report.

| KPI | Value | Impact on the business | Improvement / maintenance strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Margin** | 45.0% | Healthy product economics, but blended across three unequal channels | Report by channel; review retail wholesale terms, which carry the thinnest margin |
| **Operating Margin** | 10.0% | Sound but not generous; leaves little room for cost shocks | Protect by addressing turnover cost (36% of EBIT) before cutting marketing |
| **Net Margin** | 7.8% | What the owners retain after tax | Maintain; the gap to gross margin is where operating discipline shows |
| **ROI (year 1)** | 26.0% | Positive, but payback is 3.85 years | Always report alongside payback and cumulative ROI; re-evaluate at year 2 against forecast |
| **Blended CAC** | NOK 175.88 | **Misleading in isolation** — fell 28% purely through mix shift | Retire blended CAC as a headline; report CAC and LTV:CAC per segment |
| **LTV:CAC (D2C / B2B)** | 17.3× / 13.0× | Both segments acquire profitably | Maintain; rebuild the B2B pipeline, where new accounts fell 33% |
| **Website traffic** | 21,500 sessions/month | Growing 37.8%, but conversion is falling | Shift targets from sessions to revenue per session; audit paid-traffic intent |
| **D2C conversion rate** | 1.16% | **Down 12.4%** — the constraint on turning traffic into revenue | Checkout and product-page testing before buying more traffic |
| **Employee turnover** | 20.6% total, 11.8% regretted | NOK 665,000, or 36.1% of operating profit | Levelled production planning, upskilling route, roadmap ownership for designers |

**Three recommendations, in priority order:**

1. **Stop reporting blended CAC as a headline KPI.** Replace it with LTV:CAC per segment. The current headline says acquisition improved 28% when acquired lifetime value was flat.
2. **Fix conversion before buying more traffic.** Each additional percentage point of traffic currently returns roughly half a point of orders. Conversion work is cheaper than media spend and compounds across all existing traffic.
3. **Treat regretted design turnover as a revenue risk, not an HR metric.** Two senior designers left a 15-person function that feeds the D2C product pipeline. The cost will appear in revenue long before it appears in any HR report.

## Step 6: Reflection

Working through Nordtre made the distinction between financial and non-financial KPIs concrete in a way the definitions alone do not.

**Financial KPIs told me what happened, precisely and late.** Revenue, the three margins and ROI are auditable and unambiguous, and they are exactly the figures a bank or an owner will ask for. But every one of them is lagging. The operating margin of 10.0% is a fact about a year that has already finished. Nothing in it warned that the B2B pipeline was shrinking or that conversion was falling.

**Non-financial KPIs told me why, and early.** CAC by segment, conversion rate and regretted turnover are all leading. Each one, in this analysis, pointed at something that had not yet reached the financial statements: a mix shift that will flatten future revenue, a conversion decline that is wasting current media spend, and a designer departure that threatens the product pipeline. This is what the lesson means by non-financial metrics indirectly impacting financial health — they are the causes of numbers that have not been reported yet.

**The strongest lesson was about contextualisation.** Almost every headline figure in this activity is defensible on its own and misleading in isolation:

- "Profit margin is 45%" — until you ask which margin, and find 7.8% at the other end.
- "ROI is 26%" — until you ask over what horizon, and find payback at 3.85 years.
- "CAC improved 28%" — until you compare acquired lifetime value, and find it flat.
- "Traffic grew 38%" — until you look at conversion, and find orders grew 21%.
- "Turnover is 20.6%" — until you separate regretted from unregretted, and see which two people left.

In every case the number was arithmetically correct. What was missing was a benchmark, a prior period, a segment, or a paired KPI. A KPI is a heuristic: it simplifies a decision, and it can lead to an error. All five of those would have led to one.

**Why regular monitoring matters.** None of the findings above are visible in a single snapshot. Every one of them came from a comparison — this year against last, one segment against another, a leading indicator against its lagging partner. A KPI measured once is a number; a KPI measured repeatedly is a signal. And because thresholds and definitions drift, the KPI set itself needs periodic review: retiring blended CAC is as much a part of managing Nordtre's performance as improving it would have been.

## Formula reference

Every calculation used in this activity. The **General formula** column is the one to memorise, because cell references change between tasks while the formula does not.

### Revenue and margin

| KPI | General formula | Excel here | Reach for it when | Most common error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | Sum of all revenue streams | `=SUM(B5:B7)` | The starting figure of any financial analysis | SUM range not extended after a channel is added |
| Gross profit | Revenue − COGS | `=B8-B11` | Judging product or channel profitability | Putting operating expenses into COGS |
| Gross margin % | Gross profit ÷ Revenue × 100 | `=B12/B8*100` | Comparing products, channels, suppliers | Dividing by COGS instead of revenue |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | Revenue − Total expenses | `=B8-B20` | Judging how the company is run | Double-counting or omitting COGS |
| Operating margin % | EBIT ÷ Revenue × 100 | `=B22/B8*100` | Management performance, budget targets | Quoting it as "profit margin" beside a gross margin |
| Net profit | EBIT − tax (− interest) | `=B22-B24` | What the owners retain | Reporting pre-tax profit as net |
| Net margin % | Net profit ÷ Revenue × 100 | `=B25/B8*100` | Bottom-line and investor reporting | Mixing margin variants in one comparison |

### Investment

| KPI | General formula | Excel here | Reach for it when | Most common error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROI % | Gain from investment ÷ Cost of investment × 100 | `=B33/B32*100` | Capital allocation decisions | Using **total company profit** as the gain |
| Payback period | Investment ÷ Annual return | `=B32/B33` | Always, beside ROI, to expose the horizon | Omitting it, so a slow investment looks fast |
| Cumulative ROI % | (Annual return × years − Investment) ÷ Investment × 100 | `=(B33*3-B32)/B32*100` | Multi-year appraisal | Assuming an annual ROI simply multiplies |

### Acquisition

| KPI | General formula | Excel here | Reach for it when | Most common error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAC | Acquisition spend ÷ New customers | `=B5/C5` | Marketing efficiency, **per segment** | Using total marketing spend, including brand and retention |
| Blended CAC | **Total** spend ÷ **Total** customers | `=B7/C7` | Only alongside the segment figures | Taking the **average** of segment CACs |
| Lifetime value | AOV × Gross margin × Purchases per lifetime | `=B18*C18*D18` | Whenever CAC is reported | Using revenue instead of gross profit |
| LTV:CAC | LTV ÷ CAC | `=E18/D5` | Judging whether acquisition is sustainable (>3× rule of thumb) | Computing it blended, which hides a mix shift |

### Traffic, change and rates

| KPI | General formula | Excel here | Reach for it when | Most common error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conversion rate % | Orders ÷ Sessions × 100 | `=B33/B30*100` | Every time website traffic is reported | Orders and sessions from systems that count differently |
| Percentage change | (New − Old) ÷ **Old** × 100 | `=(B24-B25)/B25*100` | Any period-on-period comparison | Dividing by the **new** value |
| Employee turnover % | Departures ÷ **Average** headcount × 100 | `=B38/C38*100` | Organisational health, segmented | Using closing headcount instead of average |
| Cost as % of profit | Cost ÷ Operating profit × 100 | `=B44/'Financial KPIs'!B22*100` | Pricing a non-financial problem financially | Expressing it against revenue, which makes it look trivial |

## How to apply this in an exam or assignment

### The pattern almost every KPI task follows

You are given a business, some figures, and asked to compute and interpret. The marks are rarely in the arithmetic. They are in **what you compute alongside it** and **what you say about it**.

### A four-step routine

| Step | What to do | What it earns |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Compute the asked-for figure** | Use the formula, show it, state the unit | The baseline mark. Never skip showing the formula |
| **2. Compute its partner** | Every KPI in the table above has one. Revenue→margin, CAC→LTV, traffic→conversion, turnover→regretted split, ROI→payback | This is where most of the marks are |
| **3. Contextualise** | Against a prior period, a target, a benchmark, a segment, or normal variation | Turns a number into a finding |
| **4. State the decision** | Accept, accept with conditions, investigate, or reject — and what you would check next | Evaluation exists to support a decision |

### Which formula for which question

| If the task asks… | Compute | And always add |
|---|---|---|
| "Calculate the profit margin" | All three margins, or name which one you used | Why the variant matters |
| "Is this investment worthwhile?" | ROI **and** payback **and** cumulative ROI | The horizon, explicitly |
| "Evaluate marketing efficiency" | CAC per segment, LTV, LTV:CAC | That blended CAC moves with mix |
| "Has the website performed?" | Sessions, conversion rate, orders | That traffic alone is a vanity metric |
| "Assess employee turnover" | Total, by team, regretted-only, and its cost | Cost as a share of operating profit |
| "Compare this year to last" | (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100 | Whether the change exceeds normal variation |
| "Which KPIs matter most?" | Rank them | Why the obvious alternative is weaker |

### Six sentences that reliably earn marks

Adapt these to whatever business the task gives you:

1. *"Profit margin is ambiguous here; I have used operating margin, which is X%, and note that gross margin is Y%."*
2. *"ROI is X% in year one, but payback is Z years, so cumulative ROI over the appraisal period is still negative."*
3. *"CAC is only interpretable relative to lifetime value; at an LTV:CAC of N×, acquisition is sustainable in this segment."*
4. *"Blended CAC is total spend divided by total customers, not the average of the segment figures, because the segments differ in volume by a factor of N."*
5. *"Traffic rose X% while conversion fell Y%, so the traffic growth is partly waste rather than growth."*
6. *"Overall turnover of X% includes unregretted departures; regretted turnover is Y%, and that is the figure with a business consequence."*

### Sanity checks before you submit

Run these on any KPI answer. They take a minute and they catch the errors that cost most:

- Does gross margin exceed operating margin, which exceeds net margin? If not, a reference is wrong.
- Do the component figures sum to the total you reported?
- Can you reach at least one headline figure by a second, independent route?
- Is every percentage change divided by the **old** value?
- Have you stated the unit and the period for every number?
- Is any documentation note in your spreadsheet accidentally starting with `=`?

## Why each solution in the spreadsheet was reached

The workbook's **Method and Rationale** sheet records this in full. The decisions that carry the most weight:

| Decision | What I chose | Why | What I rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business design** | Three channels with different unit economics | A single-channel business makes every blended KPI correct by accident, and Step 4 would have had nothing to find | One channel |
| **Which margin** | All three, reported together | "Profit margin" is ambiguous; showing 45.0%, 10.0% and 7.8% makes the ambiguity concrete | A single figure — picking the flattering one is selective evaluation |
| **ROI numerator** | Incremental profit from the investment | Only profit **caused** by the investment belongs in the numerator | Total company profit ÷ investment — credits one machine with everything the company did |
| **ROI horizon** | Year 1, payback, and cumulative 3-year together | +26% and −22% are both correct; quoting one is a choice that changes the decision | A single ROI figure |
| **CAC granularity** | Per segment first, blended second and labelled | D2C is NOK 100, B2B is NOK 5,000; blended describes neither customer | Blended CAC as the headline |
| **Blending method** | Total spend ÷ total customers | Segments must be weighted by volume | Averaging the two CACs — wrong by 14×, and there is a reconciliation check for it |
| **Adding LTV** | LTV and LTV:CAC per segment | The lesson defines a healthy CAC *relative to lifetime value*; without LTV a falling CAC cannot be told from a shift to cheaper customers | CAC alone — it would have supported the opposite conclusion |
| **LTV formula** | AOV × **gross margin** × purchases | LTV must represent contribution, so it is comparable to CAC, which is a cost | AOV × purchases — overstates LTV by more than double |
| **Isolating the finding** | Held LTV assumptions constant across both years | Holding LTV fixed isolates the **mix** effect, proving the CAC fall is an artefact | Comparing blended CAC alone |
| **Traffic** | Sessions, uniques, duration and conversion separately | They measure activity, reach, depth and yield, and here they move by different amounts | One "traffic" number |
| **Order source** | Order system, not analytics | Two independent routes to one figure is ensembling in miniature | Analytics only — lossy and unreconcilable |
| **Turnover** | Total, by team, and regretted-only | 20.6% and 13.3% both hide that two senior designers left | Headline turnover alone |
| **Turnover denominator** | Average headcount | Does not distort when headcount changes mid-year | Closing headcount |
| **Pricing turnover** | As a share of **operating profit** (36.1%) | Converts an HR metric into one a finance audience must act on | As a share of revenue (3.6%) — makes a material cost look trivial |
| **Reconciliation checks** | Five checks that must all read OK | The worst spreadsheet errors are valid formulas pointing at wrong cells | Trusting formulas because the output looks plausible |
| **Documentation cells** | Forced to text type | A note reading `=B8-B11` becomes a live formula and silently computes something wrong | Writing notes normally — an early draft of this workbook had 15 cells doing exactly that |

## Appendix: Two errors in the supplied Activity 1.1.1 workbook

While working from the provided example I checked its formulas against its own reported figures. Two do not agree.

| Cell | Formula as supplied | What it computes | What the report text states | Correct formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Financial KPIs!B14` (ROI) | `=B2/B3*100` | Online Sales ÷ Retail Sales = **150%** | 133.3% | `=B12/B13*100` → 133.3% |
| `Financial KPIs!B19` (CAC) | `=B2/B3` | Online Sales ÷ Retail Sales = **1.5** | R30 | `=B17/B18` → 30 |

Both formulas were copied from the first calculation block without adjusting their cell references, so both point at Online Sales and Retail Sales instead of at their own inputs. The written report is correct; the spreadsheet is not.

This is worth recording because it is a textbook example of the error class this course is about. The syntax is valid. No cell shows an error. Every value in the sheet is a plausible number, and no data-quality rule — type checking, range validation, completeness — would flag either one. Only reconciling the spreadsheet against an independent statement of the same figure exposes it.

That is why the companion workbook for this activity includes a **Checks** sheet: five reconciliation tests that must all read OK, including one that verifies blended CAC was computed as total spend ÷ total customers rather than as the average of the segment CACs.
